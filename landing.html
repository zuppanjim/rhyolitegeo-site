The thesis

The long items are already done.

A conventional data center schedule is dominated by work we do not have to do. Siting at generation that is already operating removes it, item by item. Behind the meter, the path from engine to rack is measured in feet and the power never reaches transmission.

01

The generation is already permitted and running

We site at plants that cleared their own approvals years ago. The long permitting fight sits behind the host, not ahead of us.

02

The interconnection already exists

The host plant is already connected, and nobody counts it. There is no queue position to hold and no new transmission to build.

03

Utility equipment is off the critical path

The equipment with the worst lead time is the equipment these sites never order. Removes twelve to eighteen months of lead time.

04

The kit is standard, the shell is not

One interior package that never changes, engineered once rather than repeated per site, ordered ahead and assembled.

Site selection

A data problem before real estate.

A conventional search starts with a broker and the small set of buildings somebody happened to be marketing. We start from the opposite end: the whole population of candidate parcels in a region, pulled from federal, state and county records into a single keyed map, so the narrowing happens in software and field time is spent only on what survives.
